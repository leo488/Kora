# How Leo likes his designs

This guide is for the Koraa’ studio site and case studies. It was written from everything Leo has asked for and rejected across this project (Nov 2026). Read it before designing, laying out or animating anything.

## The working rules

- **Do only what was asked.** Don't design, generate or "improve" anything Leo didn't ask for. Don't invent copy, lines or posts. Use his exact words and never expand them.
- **Keep what he loves, and change only what he points at.** When he says "bring back" something, restore that exact version, not a new take on it.
- **Don't ask again for things he already gave.** Use the local files he uploads (.fig, zips, screenshots) before any connector.
- **Short replies, one step at a time, clear direction.** Lead with the result.
- **Credits are real money.** Never spend Magnific or other paid credits without first telling him the purpose and the exact cost, then waiting for his yes. Never download stock through the Magnific connector (150 credits each). Leo downloads stock himself for free.
- **Good enough to send beats perfect.** For proposals, about 10 solid frames per project is enough.

## The look

- **High end, clean and calm.** Think of the Ragged Edge Wise case study and the Surgepay case study.
- **Dark by default**, with a light toggle. The ground is #0b0b0b. Case study pages follow the site theme.
- **The layout is stacked rectangles:**
  - some plates run full width, others sit in a centred column (about 64%, max 1080px)
  - now and then, a pair of plates side by side (a small bento)
  - one 24px gutter everywhere, with neat alignment
  - no grey filler surfaces
  - no repeated images on the same page
- **Rounded plates with a light motion:** image corners straighten as they scroll in, and plates flare at the bottom of the screen (Ragged Edge). Never box shadows.
- **No clutter:**
  - no decorative circles, half-cut outlines or extra colours outside the brand palette
  - no standalone 2D illustrations
  - no wobbling 3D icons
- **Brand assets are sacred.** Never redraw, recolour or rotate a client's logo. Place the real file.
- **Type has a job.** A campaign display face (e.g. Futura LT Condensed for iPayBTC) is for headlines only, never inside app UI. App UI uses the product's own UI font.
- **App UI must be the real product UI**, alive (typing amounts, real flows), mapped onto devices in photos. No flat slid-in screens, no lookalike mock UI.

## 3D and imagery

- **3D, Surgepay style:** satin finish with fine glitter grain, two-tone gradients, soft studio light, pastel or brand-colour grounds. Use the client's own shapes (for iPayBTC, the tilted-oval coin mark).
- **Photos:** warm, sunny, real people. His Magnific taste collection: https://www.magnific.com/user/collection?id=7970487. Use the stock he links as-is, never as AI "recreations".
- **Mockups:** flat, saturated brand colour merch (tee, tote, cap, lanyard), billboards, posters on walls, building wraps, phones in hand.
- **Composite overlays yourself:** generate blank screens and blank merch, then place the real UI or logo in code.

## Motion

- **Pace:** quick moves of about 0.6 to 0.9s with a strong ease-out, short holds (about 1s, or 1.5 to 2s where text must be read), overlapping beats. Never sluggish and never "zap zap".
- **Keep it centred.** No wobble or shake, no generic fades or bounces.
- **Type swaps:** words change in place, with matching 3D objects swapping beside them.

## Following a reference page (Nov 14, Refreeg)

- When Leo gives a reference page, copy it exactly, plate for plate: the same mockup scenes, order, layout, design style, lighting and image choice, regenerated for the client. Don't invent your own mockups and don't swap in assets from the client's files.
- The client's Figma is only for the logo, colours, fonts and the screens Leo names. Don't reuse its mockups or photos as plates.
- Never put a web app or other UI on a billboard. Billboards carry a campaign design, styled like the reference.
- Use only the screens Leo asks for (for Refreeg: the web app page where the user is using the AI, plus one strong mobile page).

## Things he rejected (don't repeat)

- The animated gradient hero, the copy "Brands that refuse to blend in", thin big body type and big blue stat numbers.
- 2.5D cut-out characters with camera pans, AI video that warps phones and melts 3D, and mixed looks that read like a slideshow.
- Over-zoomed or over-cropped phone plates, and squashed icon plates.
- Generic AI recreations of his reference scenes.

## Case study recipe

1. **Hero:** the project title, then facts and a short story.
2. **The image run**, in this order:
   - a strong full-width opener (the logo or a hero mockup)
   - a logo plate
   - one or two product scenes
   - a billboard or poster
   - type and colour boards
   - merch
   - campaign and social
3. **End with** the next project and contact.
